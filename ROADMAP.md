# Marble Run course roadmap

All ten courses support two to six named racers and use fixed, pre-generated layouts. Each race has its own shuffled grid. Each course has a distinct instrumental soundtrack that loops during the heat.

## Prism Speedway

The six launch troughs descend over shallow rollers into the Spectrum Hairpin. The return straight feeds the opposing timber bank. In both turns, a marble can climb the rounded shoulder between lanes while carrying speed around the bend. The shoulders taper into the shared finishing deck, and the timing stripe sits upstream of the collection tray. A close finish uses the leading edge of the marble at the stripe. The camera follows remaining racers after the winner crosses.

Each rematch shuffles racers across the six slots, including smaller fields. With the rounded shoulders, all 500 seeded heats finished in 35.59–44.71 seconds and every slot won at every field size. In the two-marble heat with seed 1, each racer must carry at least 90% of its entry speed through the first six units of each banked turn. A single physics tick may cost at most 6% of forward speed anywhere in the turn.

## Timber Corkscrew

**Race sequence.** The timber start gate feeds a short, grooved launch. The dividers taper before a long descending helix around a central wooden drum. An outside line builds speed, while the inside line covers less distance. A broad exit leads into two opposite switchbacks, a shallow grooved descent and a short sprint into a felt-lined catch tray.

**Geometry and materials.** An open, descending helix wraps 1¼ turns around a maple drum. Its radius, pitch and clearance are baked into the course layout. Plywood edges, drum staves, brass hoops and cantilever arms frame a continuous wooden running surface. The two decks have enough clearance for six marbles to pass beneath the upper turn.

**Progress and cameras.** Both the previous track segment and the marble’s elevation identify the current deck. The camera follows the outside of the helix to keep the drum behind the pack. A downstream view picks up the exit and the trestle switchbacks.

**Acceptance.** All 500 release heats finished in 37.61–47.77 seconds. Every grid slot won at every field size. Check for jumps in displayed rank when marbles pass above each other. The inside edge cannot trap a slow marble. All five field sizes must clear the helix and the switchback joints. Review slow and fast examples from inside and outside starts; verify the drum never hides the selected marble for an entire turn.

## Wireworks Drop

**Race sequence.** A high gate releases the field onto six chutes on paired wire rails. The outside pairs start higher to compensate for their longer trip around the wire bend. The rails level out over a wooden catch deck before the field splits around a support tower. The lower, slate-blue branch in the landscape tower shot takes the short line. The longer aqua branch banks toward the tower so marbles carry speed around its wide sweep. Both feed a common banked turn and a straight finish.

**Geometry and materials.** The twelve cylindrical rails support the marbles directly. The running surface beneath each marble meets the wooden catch deck, where the rails sink into the wood. After the deck, the fork has its own floors and guards around a central tower. Both routes return to a common racing surface before the timing stripe.

**Progress and cameras.** Each branch maps its traveled distance to shared checkpoints between the split and the merge. Finish times use the marble’s physical crossing of the stripe. A side shot shows the descent. A fixed shot covers the tower and the two routes in landscape and portrait. The race audio blends in a steel-wire rattle while marbles occupy the rails.

**Acceptance.** All 500 seeded heats finished in 33.84–43.29 seconds. Every starting slot and both branches won at every field size. In 100 full-field heats the slate branch led at the merge 39 times and won 53 races. For batches of at least 100 six-marble heats, at least 80% should use both routes. Within those contested heats, each branch should lead at least 30% of merges and win at least 30% of races. Test isolated marbles on each route and six arriving together. Check the splitter and reunion for trapping or seams, and verify that the wire rattle gives way to the timber roll on the catch deck.

## Frostline Rally

**Race sequence.** The field leaves a gate in a snowy village square, enters a smooth ice-blue S-bend, crosses a short bank of wooden rollers and fans out through staggered round deflectors. A broad final skating turn leads over a striped finish bridge.

**Geometry and materials.** Six terraced starting chutes feed the Glacier Esses. Ice and wood use separate contact materials on a continuous downhill profile. Ten rounded deflectors form staggered rows in Snowcap Slalom. Opaque blue ice, dark rail edges and a red truss bridge distinguish the racing line from the snow. The clock tower, chalets and pines sit outside the channel.

**Progress and cameras.** An elevated three-quarter shot follows the S-bend. A fixed camera frames the slalom across the track in landscape and along its length in portrait. A side view covers the bridge. Track downhill progress through the wide deflector field without awarding places to marbles that bounce sideways across adjacent rows. Use dark trim at the track edges for contrast against snow.

**Acceptance.** The 500 seeded release heats finished in 38–63 seconds, including 100 six-player heats in 41–60 seconds. Verify a marble starting almost at rest can clear each transition. Test two-marble pinches at deflectors and a full six-marble pack at the skating turn. Compare speeds exiting the ice and wood sections. Check pale marble visibility, shadow cost and timing-stripe readability on a phone viewport.

## Midnight Orrery

The 201-unit race descends around a miniature solar system. Three brass pendulums swing across the Escapement Gallery at different rates. After the Retrograde Hairpin, marbles pass beneath the gallery on the Ecliptic Underpass. At Comet Skips, two curved ramps launch them across open gaps onto lower catch decks. Transparent side shields guide off-center landings. The production soundtrack pairs celesta with bass clarinet and viola.

**Cameras and acceptance.** A fixed shot frames all three pendulums. A low camera looks through the underpass, and a side view follows both jumps through the second landing. The simulation drives the visible pendulums and their kinematic colliders from the race clock. Check that replaying a seed reproduces its finish times and that the crossing resolves to the correct deck. At each jump, verify that every marble in a six-player heat rises and descends without floor support, then lands on the receiving deck. A marble can brush a side shield while airborne.

All 1,000 seeded heats finished in 50.58–77.71 seconds, and every grid slot won at every field size. In 100 six-marble heats, all 1,200 jumps landed. Airborne time ranged from 0.87–1.33 seconds on the first jump and 0.93–1.55 seconds on the second.

## Tideglass Conservatory

Six wooden chutes feed the 182-unit glasshouse race. The Propagation Esses lead around Lily Basin into the Standing Wave Gallery, where the low point of the half-pipe moves from side to side along the flume. A marble can climb a shoulder and fall back through the trough. Beyond Fernbank Turn, a raised glass ridge curves inward from the outside racing line to form the Twin Frond Sluice. The ridge tapers away before the Mist Garden and wooden Estuary Landing. Alto flute and nylon-string guitar carry the bossa nova soundtrack.

**Cameras and acceptance.** Follow the outside of the two basin turns and look across the trough from the open side of the glasshouse. Keep both sluices in view as the pack separates and rejoins. All 500 seeded heats finished in 54.41–68.05 seconds. Both sluices and every starting slot produced winners at each field size. Six-marble packs also cleared the course from rest at each section entrance.

## Kepler Relay

Six launch channels feed the Solar Array Esses. After the banked Magnetosphere turn, alternating crossfalls on Telemetry Straight shift the pack sideways. Far Side Turn lines the marbles up for Gravity Dive. The 16-unit descent reaches a 40° grade and flows into a five-unit rising lip. Each marble carries its own momentum across the eight-unit gap to Aurora Catch. Dotted side shields catch angled launches. Short trails in each racer's color trace the flight. The blue glass has a shallow landing bed and shoulders that curl up into an open half-pipe. The outside shoulder rises through each bend of Orbital Return, guiding fast marbles through the change of direction. The glass levels out toward the finish beacon. The course plays “Signal Return,” a 69.54-second instrumental with a stereo sequencer, string-synth pads and a gliding lead.

**Cameras and acceptance.** An overview frames the station against a nebula. The broadcast tracks the marbles from the side through takeoff and touchdown. An elevated rear three-quarter view follows the pack down the open glass trough. Keep the receiving lip and marbles visible in both landscape and portrait. Measure from gate release to the first finish, with a minimum of 60 seconds. For every racer, check for at least two seconds without floor support, including ascent and descent, followed by supporting contact with the glass. In the two-marble heat with seed 2, each marble must keep at least 90% of its entry forward speed throughout Orbital Return and avoid contact with the upright rim on either side.

All 500 seeded heats finished. Winners crossed in 63.72–70.01 seconds, and the final marble crossed in 65.20–76.98 seconds. All 2,000 marbles landed after 2.63–3.72 seconds airborne. Every starting slot won at every field size. Across the same 100 six-marble grids, median forward speed at the exit of Orbital Return rose from 2.73 to 6.78 units per second. One of the 600 marbles touched a vertical guard in the bends, down from 589 on the original receiver.

## Crux Mountain Masters

Six lanes drop from Belay Gate onto a charcoal grip board with a brass dome and orange bolt pads. Four gap jumps and five banked turnarounds fold the 1,195-unit course through the climbing hall. On Blade Slalom, four tapered guide bars split the ivory deck into passing channels through an S-bend. Seven faceted climbing holds on Volume Traverse deflect the field before the last checkpoint. Double-Apex Hook links two banked quarter-turns around a red elbow rail with an open route on either side. The final jump leads into the timber sprint. The original melodic house score “Contour Line” carries a four-on-the-floor kick, warm bass and a rising synth melody through the climbs. Its stereo loop runs 92 bars at 124 BPM, about 2 minutes 58 seconds.

**Race formats.** A six-marble Knockout has four elimination stations. The white ENTRY stripe spans the mouth of each funnel. Record the instant each marble’s leading edge crosses it, including crossings within the same physics tick. Five qualify at the first station, four at the second, three at the third and two at the fourth. A second guide rail catches marbles that ride up the padded funnel walls. Qualifiers keep their places if another marble passes them on the brake rollers. At the fork, an air jet lifts the last entrant above the qualifying belt onto a separate return rail. It waits there until every qualifier reaches a pad behind the bar. It then rolls into the side pocket and the qualifiers launch side by side. Smaller fields start the cuts later to leave two finalists. Open stations record entrance splits as the field races through. Endurance keeps all four gates open.

**Cameras and acceptance.** Frame the four obstacle boards and their passing lines in both orientations. Show takeoff and touchdown across each gap. At an active station, hold on the entry stripe through the final crossing, then show the seated qualifiers, the pocket and their joint restart. Run 100 seeds per field size and format. In each Knockout, compare the cut against independently measured nose crossings at the visible entrance and check a last-pair overtake inside the funnel. Verify physical passage through the throat, assigned pad positions and simultaneous releases. Every winner must take at least 120 seconds. Check for contact with all four boards, clean landings and successful restarts from each section.

All 500 Endurance and 500 Knockout heats finished on seeds 1–100. Endurance winners crossed in 140.77–151.31 seconds and six-marble Knockout winners in 160.42–169.33 seconds. Every grid slot won at every field size in both formats. All 1,000 cuts matched the observed entry order, including 38 instances in which a different marble reached the fork last. All 4,000 crossings at active gates passed inside the physical throat. In 20 six-marble heats per format, the median free-racing speed was 8.18 units per second in Knockout and 9.38 in Endurance. Those heats averaged 14.7 and 8.8 marble-to-marble impacts, counting impulses of at least 0.45 separated by more than 0.12 seconds for the same pair.

## Spectral Relay

Six marbles stage side by side on the face of the Drop Shaft. At release they fall straight down between white lane lights. A curved receiver turns the fall into a sprint toward the first timing beam. The field sweeps three quarters of a turn around the Halo Carousel turbine, crosses the Induction Gallery and winds onto the Chromatic Viaduct. Beyond Flywheel Bend, the Polarity Inverter alternates the pull over shorter stretches of track. The light on each coil follows the same traveling magnetic field that pulls the marbles across the deck. The Diffraction Esses lead into the final checkpoint and timing stripe. The ElevenLabs instrumental “Chromatic Slipstream” pairs a descending glass-synth hook with syncopated bass and electronic drums.

**Checkpoints.** Each of the five beams records the leading edge of every marble to a fraction of a physics tick. The HUD lights the numbered sector when the first racer crosses and shows their name and time. All entrants continue racing through the gates. Open **Checkpoint splits** in the results to see who led each sector, then expand a gate for the complete passing order and elapsed times.

**Cameras and acceptance.** Track the center of the falling pack through the scoop and onto Afterimage Straight. Show the bank around the turbine and the movement of the field beside the active coils. Keep the timing beam and its numbered dial readable at crossings in landscape and portrait. Run 100 seeded heats at each field size. Every winner must take at least 120 seconds from release, and every entrant must register once at each gate, in sequence, before finishing. Test a six-marble drop for straight vertical travel, smooth motion on contact with the scoop and speed out of the receiver. Restart the field from rest at each downstream section.

All 500 seeded heats finished. Winners crossed in 122.52–148.51 seconds and the last marble crossed in 133.89–189.91 seconds. All six grid slots produced winners at every field size. The five beams recorded 10,000 splits across 2,000 marble runs. In isolation, a resting marble in each magnetic gallery accelerated toward the illuminated pole.

## Afterglow FM

Six launch lanes feed Crossfader Alley, where three motorized pucks slide across the racing line at different rates. Each heat’s seed sets their starting positions, so a rematch changes the gaps the pack encounters. Carrier Spiral banks through 270° before the rainbow bands squeeze from 8.4 units wide to 3.11 on Mono Bridge, beneath the upper deck. The road opens up at Sideband Turn, then folds through Stereo Return on the way to the stripe. Every entrant races continuously from release to finish. The ElevenLabs instrumental “Dial Tone at Midnight” pairs a two-note FM synth call with a syncopated octave bass in a 65.62-second loop.

**Cameras and acceptance.** The opening shot looks back at the starting field. Crossfader Alley frames the pack alongside the sliding pucks, then the camera follows the banking through Carrier Spiral. At Mono Bridge, a landscape camera follows from upstream and a portrait camera looks back from downstream to keep the upper deck clear of the leader. Across 100 seeds at each field size, the first marble must take at least 60 seconds and the final marble must finish within 90 seconds. Verify that all six starting slots can win, that rerunning the same grid reproduces its finish times, and that every racer receives a finish time.

All 1,000 heats on seeds 1–100 and 1009–1108 finished. Winners crossed in 60.79–81.10 seconds and the final marble crossed in 62.92–86.87 seconds. Each starting slot won at every field size. A six-marble pack could start from rest at every downstream section, and each moving puck displaced a marble in a direct contact test.

## Adding courses to the game

1. Register each playable course in the setup catalog. Give it a reproducible layout seed, preview, material sections, camera shots and finish plane.
2. Reuse shared track sampling, collision construction, gates, finish detection and timing. Keep branches and layered paths explicit in their course data.
3. Add the new course to the no-render physics runner. Run at least 100 seeded grids at every field size from two to six. Preserve failing seeds and report durations, escapes, stalls and win rates per starting slot.
4. Play a complete heat and rematch through the browser UI with a custom name and color for every entrant. Verify those identities in the live order, results and saved standings. Check desktop and phone layouts, the optional race marker, each signature camera shot and sound on/off. Include close finishes and a split pack in camera reviews.
   On a phone, rotate during setup and countdown and check the grid framing. During the heat, verify the leader and marked racer in the compact running order and the full field when expanded. Exercise each course and soundtrack after an offline relaunch.
5. Keep points and wins global by color and normalized name. Once every entrant finishes or is eliminated, award 3 points for first place, 2 for second and 1 for third. Fourth through sixth earn zero. Record each completed heat once, and keep old winner-only records worth 3 points per win. A rematch reuses the selected course, format and entrants and shuffles the starting slots. Smoke-test the shipped GitHub Pages build on its repository subpath.
